# Reviewer answers: O-RAN S-plane timing security

**Evidence status:** 2026-11-03  
**Primary project baseline:** 168-run classification campaign plus the 5 October 2026, 140-run recovery-loop evaluation.  
**Reading rule:** MEASURED, CONFIGURED and REFERENCE claims are kept separate. The software testbed measured protocol state, not RF service or nanosecond clock error.

## Q1. What kind of attack, and by whom?

The project studies eight PTP timing attacks on the Open Fronthaul synchronization plane: rogue grandmaster (A1), Sync/Follow_Up spoofing (A2), replay (A3), PTP flooding (A5), rogue boundary clock (A8), interception/removal (C1), malformed frames (C2) and whole-second time-property spoofing (C3). The attacker is defined by access and position: an internal attacker or injector that can transmit on the fronthaul, or a man-in-the-middle that can intercept traffic. Realistic devices include a compromised O-RU/O-DU, switch or boundary clock, or an unauthorized host attached to the Ethernet segment. ETSI also names actor categories—insiders, nation states, cyber-criminals, hacktivists, cyber-terrorists and script kiddies—but the experiment tests capabilities, not attribution to a named person.

**Evidence:** `CITATION_VERIFICATION_2026-11.md` S09-01/S09-02; RFC 7384 §3.1: “Internal attackers either have access to a trusted segment …”; ETSI TR 104 106 §7.2; `00_LATEST_PRESENTED_DECK_AND_DELIVERABLES/ORAN_SPlane_Packets_to_Classification_2026-10-05.xlsx`.

**Limits:** The attackers are Linux namespaces on one host. The project did not compromise commercial equipment or identify a real threat actor.

## Q2. What references support the work?

The main primary references are RFC 7384 for PTP threat classes and security requirements; ETSI TR 104 106 for O-RAN threat agents and T-SPLANE threats; ETSI TS 104 105 for O-RAN security test families; ETSI TS 103 982 and TS 103 859 for architecture and the C/U/S-plane boundary; ITU-T G.8271.1 and G.8275.1 for timing budgets and the telecom PTP profile; and 3GPP TS 38.104 for radio TAE limits. TIMESAFE supplies published experimental impact, while the Armstrong and Ruffini tutorials expose O-RAN/3GPP timing concepts in a concise form. Public copies, URLs, retrieval dates, byte sizes and SHA-256 hashes are recorded locally. IEEE 1588-2019 remains paywalled and is explicitly marked not accessible.

**Evidence:** `01_CURRENT_SPlane_SelfHealing/literature-survey/papers/Standards/SOURCES_MANIFEST.md`; `CITATION_VERIFICATION_2026-11.md` (63 CONFIRMED, 2 WRONG_CLAUSE, 3 NOT_SUPPORTED, 14 NOT_ACCESSIBLE).

**Limits:** Clause-level IEEE 1588 claims require licensed access before publication. Secondary tutorials are not substitutes for normative text where safety-critical compliance is asserted.

## Q3. Does it only work on O-RAN or another architecture too?

The method is PTP-oriented, not inherently O-RAN-only. Message legality, sequence continuity, delivered-versus-declared rate, identity allow-lists and maintenance context can transfer to other networks that use IEEE 1588, including telecom, power, broadcast/audio and industrial profiles. Profile constants must be re-derived: domain, multicast addresses, message cadence and clock-selection rules differ. This project demonstrated only the ITU-T G.8275.1 full-timing-support profile in an O-RAN-like fronthaul topology.

**Evidence:** `CITATION_VERIFICATION_2026-11.md` S40-01–S40-03; RFC 7384 scope; ITU-T G.8275.1 §§6.1–6.3; v8 slide 40.

**Limits:** No non-O-RAN deployment or non-G.8275.1 profile was executed, so portability is an engineering proposition rather than a measured result.

## Q4. From which model did we get the KPIs?

No model generates the KPIs. Wire fields come from captured PTP messages, daemon timing values come from `ptp4l` logs, management state comes from `pmc`, and operator intent comes from provisioned `context.json`/state. The rule arm consumes protocol and context evidence; the ML arm consumes six servo-derived features. Thus the model is a consumer of KPIs, not their source.

**Evidence:** v8 slide 20; `00_LATEST_PRESENTED_DECK_AND_DELIVERABLES/verification_records/V8_SLIDE_SOURCE_MAP.md`; `01_CURRENT_SPlane_SelfHealing/oran_splane_selfhealing/ml_comparison_output/ML_VS_RULE_COMPARISON.md`; linuxptp `pmc.8` (`-d` domain option) confirmed in `CITATION_VERIFICATION_2026-11.md` S20-02.

**Limits:** Campaign `pmc` queries omitted `-d 24` and returned no data; the 5 October observer corrected this. Servo features remain software-timestamped and do not establish hardware-grade nanosecond accuracy.

## Q5. Where did the dataset come from?

The principal dataset was generated by the team on a six-daemon linuxptp 4.0 software testbed using two Linux bridges and network namespaces. The corrected campaign contains 168 runs: 14 scenarios × 12 repetitions, with 96 attack, 60 benign and 12 ambiguous runs. It stores packet-derived tables, context, decisions and RU daemon logs; `EVALUATION_V4.json` is the primary evaluation record. TIMESAFE captures are separate external real-hardware reference data and were not passed off as the project's own campaign.

**Evidence:** `01_CURRENT_SPlane_SelfHealing/oran_splane_selfhealing/gap_coverage_2026-09-20/corrected_final/EVALUATION_V4.json` (`n_runs=168` and per-scenario counts); `00_LATEST_PRESENTED_DECK_AND_DELIVERABLES/verification_records/SESSION_RECORD_2026-10-03.md` §2 and `V8_SLIDE_SOURCE_MAP.md` (recording the unversioned workspace `dataset/DATASET_MANIFEST.csv`); v8 slides 22–23.

**Limits:** The campaign is a software testbed on one host, not a production O-RAN network. Several archived synthetic/older datasets are superseded and must not be pooled with this result.

## Q6. Who performs these attacks and what are their impacts?

An internal injector can execute A1, A2, A3, A5, C2 or C3 after gaining transmit access; an in-path device can execute C1 or act as the A8 relay. Measured impact is protocol-specific: A1 and C3 captured RU1/RU2 for a median 38.5 seconds in the recovery control arm, while sustained C1 left them unhealthy for 39.0 seconds. A2, A3, A5, A8 and C2 caused 0 seconds of measured primary-RU impact in that topology, although the loop contained their ingress. Published service effects such as cell drop or RU crash are reference evidence, not outcomes measured by this project.

**Evidence:** `IMPACT_EVIDENCE_SCOPE.md`; `03_RECOVERY_LOOP_S-PLANE/RESULTS_2026-10-05.md` §§1–2; TIMESAFE pp. 12–13; ETSI TR 104 106 T-SPLANE-01…04.

**Limits:** `free_running 1` prevented physical clock steering. No UE throughput, RF, cell availability or absolute time-error outcome was measured.

## Q7. Again, who does these attacks and how?

The experimental actor is a host or network function with the required network position. A1/A8 run additional real `ptp4l` clocks; A2/C2/C3 inject crafted PTP frames; A3 records and replays live frames; A5 sends high-rate bursts; C1 blackholes the boundary clock's downstream port. These are executed packet-path mechanisms, not simulated labels. The corresponding real-world actor would need compromised or unauthorized access to an O-RU/O-DU, a fronthaul switch/clock, or the Ethernet link.

**Evidence:** v8 slides 10 and 24; campaign archive `splane_campaign_CORRECTED_2026-09-20.tgz` (`run/scenarios.sh`, `run/run_gap.sh`, `run/inject_malformed.py`, `run/inject_wholesecond.py`) and `03_RECOVERY_LOOP_S-PLANE/code/FROZEN_RL.json`; RFC 7384 §3.1.2.

**Limits:** The testbed does not demonstrate how initial compromise is achieved. It starts after the actor has the stated access.

## Q8. Were the eight attacks existing or made up? Where were they referred from?

All eight belong to published threat categories. A1/A8 map to rogue-master behavior, A2/C3 to spoofing, A3 to replay, A5 to time-protocol DoS, C1 to interception/removal, and C2 to exploitation/unexpected-input testing. C3's exact `leap61/currentUtcOffset` combination and C2's exact malformed values are project constructions inside those published categories. ETSI's unexpected-input test does not prescribe the same three C2 mutations, so the deck must not imply an exact reproduction.

**Evidence:** RFC 7384 §§3.2.2–3.2.5, 3.2.9, 3.2.11; ETSI TR 104 106 T-SPLANE-01…04; ETSI TS 104 105 §§11.1.5 and 24.2.1; `CITATION_VERIFICATION_2026-11.md` S11-01–S11-10.

**Limits:** IEEE 1588 §8.2.4 was not independently verified because the standard is paywalled.

## Q10. Explain the DU layers and architecture; what attacks occur there and who performs them?

The O-CU-CP hosts RRC and control-plane PDCP; the O-CU-UP hosts SDAP and user-plane PDCP; the O-DU hosts RLC, MAC and High-PHY; and the O-RU hosts Low-PHY and RF. Open Fronthaul split 7-2x connects O-DU to O-RU and carries C, U, S and M planes. This project operates on the S-plane timing path and emulates an LLS-C3 structure, where the O-DU is not in the synchronization chain; it does not emulate O-DU RLC/MAC/High-PHY behavior. The attacks therefore hit PTP processing, parent selection, time properties or message availability at the O-RU/clock path, performed by a transmitter or in-path device with fronthaul access.

**Evidence:** ETSI TS 103 982 §§3.1, 6.3.6, 6.4.7; ETSI TS 103 859 §11.2.2; `CITATION_VERIFICATION_2026-11.md` S04-01, S05-01, S06-01; v8 slides 4–6.

**Limits:** No real O-DU, C-plane, U-plane, scheduler, RF chain or 5G core was exercised.

## Q12. Which module will these attacks affect?

A1/A8 affect BMCA parent selection and the timing hierarchy; A2/A3 affect Sync/Follow_Up consumption and sequence/timestamp consistency; A5 affects PTP processing capacity; C1 affects message availability and timeout/holdover behavior; C2 affects the PTP parser/stack; C3 affects Announce time-properties and parent selection. In O-RAN terms the target is the Open Fronthaul S-plane termination and timing behavior at the O-RU and intervening clocks. The project did not demonstrate direct effects on RLC, MAC, High-PHY, Low-PHY signal processing, C-plane or U-plane traffic.

**Evidence:** `IMPACT_EVIDENCE_SCOPE.md`; `ORAN_SPlane_Packets_to_Classification_2026-10-05.xlsx` Source/Destination columns; ETSI TS 103 982 §6.3.6.

**Limits:** Literature links timing failure to radio service, but this testbed stops at protocol-state evidence.

## Q13. Classify the KPIs so recovery can proceed.

Recovery uses six evidence classes. Protocol-legality KPIs find malformed traffic; identity/topology KPIs find unauthorized GM/BC/source identities; continuity/rate KPIs find replay, flooding or removal; time-property KPIs find C3; operator context separates planned from unplanned changes; timing-quality KPIs grade severity. The first four can drive ATTACK/isolation, context can produce BENIGN/tolerate or UNKNOWN/escalate, and timing quality alone does not determine malicious intent. GNSS, SyncE and independent oscillator evidence are unavailable in this software testbed.

**Evidence:** v8 slide 21; `03_RECOVERY_LOOP_S-PLANE/RECOVERY_LOOP_DESIGN.md`; campaign archive `splane_campaign_CORRECTED_2026-09-20.tgz` (`run/decision_rule.py` and `run/decision_rule_v3.py`).

**Limits:** The classification is tied to the frozen G.8275.1 feature set. Hardware-only evidence requires a different apparatus.

## Q14. What is the source of attack?

“Source” has two meanings and both are now explicit. The network source is the ingress device/port: rogue clock namespace, RU-side injector, two-port relay or blackholed BC port. The knowledge source is the published threat/test family: RFC 7384, ETSI TR 104 106, ETSI TS 104 105 and TIMESAFE. Per-run context and packet identity locate the experimental source; the literature does not identify a real attacker in this project.

**Evidence:** 2026-10-05 workbook `Source (of attack or fault)` and `Attack devices` columns; v8 slides 10–11; `CITATION_VERIFICATION_2026-11.md`.

**Limits:** Source localisation is within the two-bridge testbed. It does not prove attribution across a production supply chain or remote compromise path.

## Q15. What are the attacks, where and how exactly do they impact?

The eight attacks and exact measured effects are tabulated in `IMPACT_EVIDENCE_SCOPE.md`. A1 and C3 changed the primary RUs' followed source for 38.5 seconds median in control; C1 removed their usable master for 39.0 seconds median. A2, A3, A5, A8 and C2 were observable/containable but produced 0 seconds primary-RU protocol-health impact in the recovery control arm. The loop reduced A1/C3 median unhealthy time to 2.0 seconds and C1 to 2.5 seconds, restoring 5/5 in each case.

**Evidence:** `03_RECOVERY_LOOP_S-PLANE/RESULTS_2026-10-05.md` §1; `IMPACT_EVIDENCE_SCOPE.md`; raw `results/recovery_eval_runs_r13-r17.tgz`; `EVALUATION_RL.json`.

**Limits:** These figures measure parent/grandmaster health, not clock offset, time above 1.5 µs, packet-service quality, RF or UE experience.

## Q16. Is the approach reactive or proactive?

The implemented loop is reactive: it acts after packet evidence or timing-service loss appears. Preventive provisioning—allow-lists, port roles, maintenance context, a standby BC and frozen rules—makes the reactive decision safer and faster. It is not predictive: no model forecasts an attack before its first observable action. Slow oscillator degradation could be trended in future, but B6 forecasting was not implemented or evaluated.

**Evidence:** `03_RECOVERY_LOOP_S-PLANE/REACTIVE_VS_PROACTIVE.md`; `RECOVERY_LOOP_DESIGN.md`; `PREREGISTRATION.md`; RFC 7384 §5.9 redundancy quote in `CITATION_VERIFICATION_2026-11.md` D08.

**Limits:** The evaluation has five repetitions per arm/scenario and one software host; it does not establish production forecasting or general proactive autonomy.

## Superseded delivery-status statement

V8 slide 38 states “closed-loop healing not validated” under M5, “None in the 168-run campaign” under Automated corrective action, and “No MTTR, recovery-time, availability or downtime figure was measured on this testbed.” Those statements described the earlier 168-run classification campaign and are superseded by the 5 October recovery-loop evaluation. The later evaluation executed 140 live runs; restored 5/5 for every attack scenario; reduced A1 and C3 median primary-RU unhealthy time from 38.5 s to 2.0 s; reduced sustained C1 from 39.0 s to 2.5 s; and verified 35/35 isolations plus 6/6 standby activations in the artifacts. This is closed-loop protocol-state recovery, not nanosecond time-error recovery or digital-twin rehearsal.

**Evidence:** `03_RECOVERY_LOOP_S-PLANE/RESULTS_2026-10-05.md` §§1, 2 and 5; `PREREGISTRATION.md`; `code/FROZEN_RL.json`.

**Limits:** H3 failed because one of 25 benign loop runs caused a disruptive standby action during a genuine B3 timing outage. The sample is small, clock steering remained disabled, and no real O-RAN service was measured.
