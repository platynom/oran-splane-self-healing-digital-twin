## 1. `S06-02` — `WRONG_CLAUSE` (LLS-C1 / `T-FRHAUL-01`)

- **Location:**
  - `00_LATEST_PRESENTED_DECK_AND_DELIVERABLES/ORAN_SPlane_PRISM_Review_v8.pptx` (and `ORAN_SPlane_PRISM_Review_v8_view.pdf`), Slide 6 (*"How the O-RU Receives Time: LLS-C1 to LLS-C4"*), row `LLS-C1`, column `WHERE AN ATTACKER CAN ACT`.
  - `00_LATEST_PRESENTED_DECK_AND_DELIVERABLES/verification_records/V8_SLIDE_SOURCE_MAP.md`, Slide 6 row (line 36).
- **Current wording:**
  - **Slide 6 (`LLS-C1` / `WHERE AN ATTACKER CAN ACT`):**
    > `A compromised O-DU or the O-DU–O-RU link controls the O-RU's time (T-FRHAUL-01 [2]).`
  - **`V8_SLIDE_SOURCE_MAP.md` (line 36):**
    > `ETSI TR 104 106 cl. 7.4.1.2 T-FRHAUL-01`
- **Why it is wrong (with quote and page):**
  In `ETSI_TR_104106_V3.0.0.pdf` (clause 7.4.1.2, PDF page 33), `T-FRHAUL-01` describes an attacker penetrating northbound into the O-DU and beyond via the O-RU or Open Fronthaul management bridging—not a compromised O-DU or link controlling O-RU timing. In `ETSI TR 104 106 V3.0.0`, physical fronthaul link access is `T-FRHAUL-02` (PDF page 33) and S-plane timing threats are `T-SPLANE-01` through `T-SPLANE-04` (PDF pages 34–35).
  - **Verbatim quote (`ETSI TR 104 106 V3.0.0`, clause 7.4.1.2, PDF page 33):**
    > *"Threat ID: T-FRHAUL-01 | Threat title: An attacker penetrates O-DU and beyond through O-RU or the Fronthaul interface [i.12] | Threat description: When having two different vendors, the O-RU and the O-DU needs to be managed as different entities and may have heterogeneous security levels. Instead, the O-DU will have to bridge the management traffic between the management system and the O-RU. Hence the possibilities to reach the northbound systems beyond the O-DU through the Open Fronthaul interface become a possible attack vector in this split architecture."*
- **Exact corrected wording:**
  - **Slide 6 (`LLS-C1` / `WHERE AN ATTACKER CAN ACT`):**
    > `A compromised O-DU (acting as synchronization master in LLS-C1) or an attacker on the O-DU–O-RU link can control or disrupt the O-RU's time (threat analysis from LLS-C1 topology; cf. T-FRHAUL-02 and T-SPLANE-01…04 [2], RFC 7384 §3.2 [1]).`
  - **`V8_SLIDE_SOURCE_MAP.md` (line 36):**
    > `ETSI TR 104 106 cl. 7.4.1.2 T-FRHAUL-02 and T-SPLANE-01…04 (LLS-C1 exposure reasoned from topology; T-FRHAUL-01 removed as wrong clause)`

## 2. `S09-04` — `NOT_SUPPORTED` (TIMESAFE §4 Access Paths on Slide 9)

- **Location:**
  - `00_LATEST_PRESENTED_DECK_AND_DELIVERABLES/ORAN_SPlane_PRISM_Review_v8.pptx` (and `ORAN_SPlane_PRISM_Review_v8_view.pdf`), Slide 9 (*"Who Attacks the S-Plane: Agents and Classes"*), lower-right block `"HOW THEY GET ON THE FRONTHAUL ([10] sec. 4; [2] T-FRHAUL-02)"`.
  - `00_LATEST_PRESENTED_DECK_AND_DELIVERABLES/verification_records/V8_SLIDE_SOURCE_MAP.md`, Slide 9 row (line 39).
- **Current wording:**
  - **Slide 9 (`HOW THEY GET ON THE FRONTHAUL`):**
    > `HOW THEY GET ON THE FRONTHAUL ([10] sec. 4; [2] T-FRHAUL-02)`  
    > `• Remote access widened by multi-vendor, disaggregated equipment`  
    > `• Physical access to edge cell sites hosting O-RU / O-DU`  
    > `• Supply-chain backdoors in DU or RU equipment`  
    > `• Unauthorized access to the Open Fronthaul Ethernet interface`
  - **`V8_SLIDE_SOURCE_MAP.md` (line 39):**
    > `TIMESAFE sec. 4 (four real-world access paths)`
- **Why it is wrong (with quote and page):**
  `TIMESAFE` (`TIMESAFE_arXiv_2412.13049v3.pdf`, Section 4 *"Threat model"*, PDF pages 8–9) discusses **three** threat-model factors that make compromising a device on the switched fronthaul plausible (*Increased Remote Access*, *Increased Physical Access*, and *Dependency and Supply Chain Vulnerabilities*, citing external reports [37, 55] for supply-chain backdoors). It does **not** enumerate all four bullets: the fourth bullet (*"Unauthorized access to the Open Fronthaul Ethernet interface"*) comes from `ETSI TR 104 106 V3.0.0` `T-FRHAUL-02` (PDF page 33). Attributing all four paths to `TIMESAFE` §4 in `V8_SLIDE_SOURCE_MAP.md` (or leaving the four bullets unattributed individually on Slide 9) overstates what `TIMESAFE` §4 directly establishes.
  - **Verbatim quote (`TIMESAFE_arXiv_2412.13049v3.pdf`, Section 4, PDF page 9):**
    > *"• Increased Remote Access. With its open and multi-vendor architecture, O-RAN relies on a diverse range of suppliers, developers, and service providers, each with varying levels of access and control over networked components. ... • Increased Physical Access. The distributed nature of gNB edge servers, often located in accessible public spaces such as sidewalks, rooftops, or building basements, makes on-site physical access an increasingly viable entry point for attacks on the FH [55]. ... • Dependency and Supply Chain Vulnerabilities. Software-based network functions in 5G, including those in Open RAN, are susceptible to supply chain attacks, particularly with the use of unvetted and globally sourced components [37]. A recent government report highlighted that supply chain risks from untrusted hardware and software vendors are significant threats, as adversarial actors could embed backdoors in either the DU or RU to manipulate fronthaul traffic without being detected by 802.1X [55]. ... The combination of increased remote access to DUs and physical access to the FH, along with dependency and supply chain vulnerabilities, makes gaining access to a device connected to the FH network not just plausible but increasingly likely [37]."*
  - **Verbatim quote (`ETSI TR 104 106 V3.0.0`, clause 7.4.1.2, PDF page 33):**
    > *"Threat ID: T-FRHAUL-02 | Threat title: Unauthorized access to Open Front Haul Ethernet L1 physical layer interface(s)"*
- **Exact corrected wording:**
  - **Slide 9 (`HOW THEY GET ON THE FRONTHAUL`):**
    > `HOW THEY GET ON THE FRONTHAUL (threat-model synthesis from [10] §4 and [2] T-FRHAUL-02)`  
    > `• Remote access widened by multi-vendor, disaggregated equipment ([10] §4)`  
    > `• Physical access to edge cell sites hosting O-RU / O-DU ([10] §4)`  
    > `• Dependency and supply-chain vulnerabilities in DU or RU equipment ([10] §4, citing [37, 55])`  
    > `• Unauthorized access to the Open Fronthaul Ethernet L1 physical interface ([2] T-FRHAUL-02)`
  - **`V8_SLIDE_SOURCE_MAP.md` (line 39):**
    > `TIMESAFE sec. 4 (three access factors: remote access, physical access, dependency/supply-chain risk); ETSI TR 104 106 cl. 7.4.1.2 T-FRHAUL-02 (fourth bullet: L1 physical interface access)`

## 3. `S11-10` — `NOT_SUPPORTED` (`C2` Three-Variant Subset vs. `ETSI TS 104 105` Test `24.2.1.2`)

- **Location:**
  - `00_LATEST_PRESENTED_DECK_AND_DELIVERABLES/ORAN_SPlane_PRISM_Review_v8.pptx` (and `ORAN_SPlane_PRISM_Review_v8_view.pdf`), Slide 11 (*"Where Each of the Eight Attacks Comes From"*), row `C2`, and Slide 26 (*"Attack Scenarios: Relay, Interception and Field Abuse"*), row `C2` plus Slide 26 speaker notes (`ppt/notesSlides/notesSlide14.xml`).
- **Current wording:**
  - **Slide 11 (`C2` row):**
    > `ID: C2 | ATTACK: Malformed frames | PUBLISHED THREAT: RFC 7384 §3.2.11 exploiting vulnerabilities | PUBLISHED TEST OR STUDY: TS 104 105 §24.2.1.2 S-Plane PTP Unexpected Input | WHAT WE CHOSE: versionPTP 3, reserved type, short length`
  - **Slide 26 (`C2` row):**
    > `SCENARIO: C2 Malformed / fuzzed frames WG11 24.2.1.2 | INJECTED CONDITION: Frames injected with versionPTP 3, a reserved message type, and a message length shorter than the protocol permits.`
  - **Slide 26 speaker notes (`notesSlide14.xml`):**
    > `Standards references for the injected conditions: C2 malformed frames map to O-RAN WG11 test 24.2.1.2...`
- **Why it is wrong (with quote and page):**
  `ETSI TS 104 105 V7.0.0` clause `24.2.1.2` (*"S-Plane PTP Unexpected Input"*, PDF pages 225–226) specifies an O-DU S-Plane robustness fuzzing test that captures a legitimate PTP message and replays at least 250,000 randomly mutated iterations toward the O-DU while checking that UE/user-plane service degradation is not noticeable. It does **not** specify the three deterministic malformed header variants (`versionPTP 3`, reserved `messageType`, truncated `messageLength`) injected on the RU segment in `C2`. Thus `C2` is a project-defined 3-variant deterministic subset inspired by the unexpected-input category, not a reproduction of ETSI test `24.2.1.2`.
  - **Verbatim quote (`ETSI TS 104 105 V7.0.0`, clause 24.2.1.2, PDF pages 225–226):**
    > *"24.2.1.2 S-Plane PTP Unexpected Input | Requirement Name: O-DU S-Plane Robustness ... Purpose: To verify that an unexpected (not in-line with protocol specification) input sent towards O-DU S-Plane will not compromise the security of the SUT. ... Test procedure: 1) The tester uses a packet capture tool to capture sample of legitimate PTP message sent towards the O-DU S-Plane. 2) The tester uses fuzzing tool to replay the captured PTP message while mutating its content and keeping original source/destination MAC address. Send at least 250 000 iterations of mutated PTP message based on a random seed. Expected results: During the execution of the test, the degradation of service availability and performance of the SUT is not noticeable."*
- **Exact corrected wording:**
  - **Slide 11 (`C2` row, `PUBLISHED TEST OR STUDY` and `WHAT WE CHOSE` columns):**
    > `PUBLISHED TEST OR STUDY: Category related to TS 104 105 §24.2.1.2 S-Plane PTP Unexpected Input (O-DU 250,000-iteration fuzz test)`  
    > `WHAT WE CHOSE: Project-defined 3-variant deterministic subset (versionPTP 3, reserved type, short length; not a reproduction of §24.2.1.2)`
  - **Slide 26 (`C2` row, `SCENARIO` and `INJECTED CONDITION` columns) and speaker notes:**
    > `SCENARIO: C2 Malformed frames (project 3-variant subset; cf. WG11 24.2.1.2 unexpected-input category)`  
    > `INJECTED CONDITION: Three deterministic project-constructed malformed variants injected with versionPTP 3, a reserved message type, and a message length shorter than the protocol permits (distinct from ETSI TS 104 105 §24.2.1.2's 250,000-iteration random-mutation O-DU fuzz test).`

## 4. `S26-04` — `NOT_SUPPORTED` (`C3` One-Second Phase/UTC Shift Not Measured Under `free_running 1`)

- **Location:**
  - `00_LATEST_PRESENTED_DECK_AND_DELIVERABLES/ORAN_SPlane_PRISM_Review_v8.pptx` (and `ORAN_SPlane_PRISM_Review_v8_view.pdf`), Slide 26 (*"Attack Scenarios: Relay, Interception and Field Abuse"*), row `C3` (`Whole-second field abuse`), column `OBSERVABLE EFFECT`.
- **Current wording:**
  - **Slide 26 (`C3` row, `OBSERVABLE EFFECT` column):**
    > `Time properties become internally inconsistent, which would shift the applied UTC offset by a whole second.`
- **Why it is wrong (with quote and page):**
  All `ptp4l` daemons in both the 168-run classification campaign and the 140-run recovery-loop evaluation ran with `free_running 1` (`cfg/g87251.base`), and IEEE Std 1588-2019 §§8.2.4, 9.4 is paywalled (`NOT_ACCESSIBLE` in `SOURCES_MANIFEST.md`). Because `free_running 1` instructs `ptp4l` not to adjust the local clock (`linuxptp_v4.0_ptp4l.8`), no physical clock steering or 1-second phase/UTC shift was ever executed or measured in this testbed. The measured effect of `C3` is protocol-state inconsistency in the Announce time-properties fields (`leap61 = 1`, `currentUtcOffset = 0` vs. `37`, traceability flags stripped) and primary-RU re-parenting to the forger (`38.5 s` median in the 5 October control arm).
  - **Verbatim quote (`linuxptp_v4.0_ptp4l.8`, CONFIGURATION FILE / General Options, lines 124–126):**
    > *"free_running: Don't adjust the local clock if set to 1. The default is 0."*
  - **Verbatim quote (`03_RECOVERY_LOOP_S-PLANE/RESULTS_2026-10-05.md`, §5 Scope and Limitations, line 64):**
    > *"1. `free_running 1` remains enabled on all `ptp4l` daemons (`cfg/g87251.base`), so physical host clocks are never steered. Recovery metrics measure **protocol-level parent/grandmaster health and time-properties validity**, not physical nanosecond phase error."*
- **Exact corrected wording:**
  - **Slide 26 (`C3` row, `OBSERVABLE EFFECT` column):**
    > `Measured on testbed: Announce time-properties fields become internally inconsistent (leap61=1, currentUtcOffset=0 vs 37, traceability flags stripped) and RUs re-parent to the forger (38.5 s median in 5 Oct control). Physical 1-second UTC/phase shift was not measured because all daemons ran with free_running 1.`

## 5. `D05` — `WRONG_CLAUSE` (`ITU-T G.8275.1` Attribute Name `timeTransmitterOnly` / `notTimeTransmitter`, Not `notSlave`)

- **Location:**
  - `03_RECOVERY_LOOP_S-PLANE/STANDARDS_EVIDENCE.md`, §1 (*"`ISOLATE_PTP_AT_PORT` — Port-Role Restriction and Acceptable-Master Gating"*), bullet 4 (line 21).
- **Current wording:**
  - **`03_RECOVERY_LOOP_S-PLANE/STANDARDS_EVIDENCE.md` (line 21):**
    > `- **ITU-T G.8275.1 (`notSlave` / `masterOnly` port attribute):** In G.8275.1, downstream ports of a T-GM or T-BC facing end-devices are provisioned with `notSlave = TRUE` (`masterOnly`), ensuring a downstream port can never transition to `SLAVE` state even if a superior `Announce` arrives on that port [Rodrigues, *ITU-T G.8275.1 Telecom Profile*, IEEE/ISPCS tutorial slides, p. 11].`
- **Why it is wrong (with quote and page):**
  The secondary Rodrigues tutorial slides are not stored in the verified standards corpus (`SOURCES_MANIFEST.md`), whereas the stored primary specification `ITU_T_G.8275.1_2026.pdf` (Recommendation ITU-T G.8275.1, 02/2026, clause 6.3.1 *"Alternate BTCA"*, PDF page 21 / Rec. page 13, and Table 3, PDF page 24 / Rec. page 16) defines the per-port Boolean attributes as `timeTransmitterOnly` and `notTimeTransmitter` (referencing Annex E of ITU-T G.8275; earlier editions used `masterOnly` / `notMaster`), **not** `notSlave`.
  - **Verbatim quote (`ITU_T_G.8275.1_2026.pdf`, clause 6.3.1, PDF page 21 / Rec. page 13):**
    > *"This Alternate BTCA differs from the default BTCA of [IEEE 1588] as follows: a) This profile uses the per-port Boolean attribute timeTransmitterOnly as specified in Annex E of [ITU-T G.8275]. b) The profile uses the per-port Boolean attribute notTimeTransmitter as specified in Annex E of [ITU-T G.8275]."*
  - **Verbatim quote (`ITU_T_G.8275.1_2026.pdf`, clause 6.3.1, Table 3, PDF page 24 / Rec. page 16):**
    > *"Table 3 – Combination of timeTransmitterOnly and notTimeTransmitter values | timeTransmitterOnly = TRUE, notTimeTransmitter = FALSE: The port is primarily intended to be in the MASTER state (ignoring localPriority). ... timeTransmitterOnly = FALSE, notTimeTransmitter = TRUE: The port is never intended to be placed in the MASTER state, applicable to peer-to-peer ports only."*
- **Exact corrected wording:**
  - **`03_RECOVERY_LOOP_S-PLANE/STANDARDS_EVIDENCE.md` (§1, bullet 4, line 21):**
    > `- **ITU-T G.8275.1 (`timeTransmitterOnly` / `notTimeTransmitter` per-port attributes):** In ITU-T G.8275.1 (02/2026), clause 6.3.1 and Table 3 (PDF pp. 21, 24), the Alternate BTCA uses the per-port Boolean attributes `timeTransmitterOnly` and `notTimeTransmitter` (as specified in Annex E of ITU-T G.8275): setting `timeTransmitterOnly = TRUE` with `notTimeTransmitter = FALSE` designates a downstream T-GM/T-BC port as primarily intended to remain in the `MASTER` state rather than synchronizing as a `SLAVE` to downstream traffic, while `notTimeTransmitter = TRUE` (applicable to peer-to-peer ports) prevents a port from entering the `MASTER` state.`
