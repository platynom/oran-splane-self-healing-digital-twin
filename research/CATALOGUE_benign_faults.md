# Benign Fault & Degradation Catalogue — Telecom PTP / Sync Networks
### Reference material for O-RAN Open Fronthaul S-plane attack-vs-benign discrimination

**Scope note:** every entry below is a *non-malicious* event that produces an observable S-plane signature. 58 scenarios in 8 families, plus 12 documented real-world incidents. Where a number is quoted it comes from the cited source; where I was unsure of an exact spec value I have said so rather than guess.

**G.8275.1 clockClass ladder** (the single most important benign-transition signal):

| clockClass | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 6 | T-GM locked to PRTC, time traceable |
| 7 | T-GM in holdover, **within** spec, still traceable |
| 52 | T-GM holdover **out of** spec (frequency not traceable) |
| 135 | T-BC holdover within spec |
| 140 / 150 / 160 | Holdover out of spec, graded by SyncE QL |
| 165 | T-BC in failed holdover |
| 248 | Default — no time reference since startup |
| 255 | Slave-only clock |

Sources: [Skyworks AN1202](https://www.skyworksinc.com/-/media/Skyworks/SL/documents/public/application-notes/an1202-itu-t_precision_time_protocol_grandmaster.pdf), [Calnex G.8275.1 summary](https://calnexsolutions.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GDW/pages/10485787/ITU-T+G.8275.1+(PTP+Profile+-+FTS)+One-Page+Summary). Practical implementations treat **{6, 7, 135} = locked-eligible** and **{140, 248, 255} = holdover/freerun** ([StarlingX PTP notification states](https://docs.starlingx.io/guest_integration/kubernetes/ptp-notification-status-conditions-6d6105fccf10.html)).

## 1. Grandmaster events

| ID | Scenario | Cause | Observable S-plane / telemetry change | Mistakable for | How operators distinguish |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B-01** | Planned GM failover (forced switch) | Maintenance, load rebalancing, operator-initiated switchover | New `grandmasterIdentity` in Announce; brief UNCALIBRATED→SLAVE; small phase step; no clockClass degradation (both GMs class 6) | Rogue-master / BMCA-takeover attack (TIMESAFE Announce spoof) | Change ticket correlation; new GM's clockIdentity is on the **known GM inventory**; Announce arrives on the expected physical port/VLAN; attributes are *worse or equal*, not spoof-perfect; GM count in domain unchanged |
| **B-02** | Unplanned GM failover | GM PSU/card/fan failure, chassis crash | Announce timeout (3× interval) on old GM → BMCA reselect; downstream clockClass may pass through 7→52 before alternate GM wins; `announce_timeout` counters increment | Announce DoS / selective Announce suppression | Old GM disappears from **all** ports simultaneously and stays gone; GM's own management plane also dead (NETCONF/SNMP down); no unexplained new clockIdentity appears |
| **B-03** | GM cold reboot | Power cycle, watchdog, upgrade reload | GM re-emits Announce with **clockClass 248** first, then 52/7, then 6 after GNSS lock and oscillator settling; GM identity unchanged | Spoofer injecting degraded-quality Announce to force reselect | The 248→52→7→6 **monotonic improvement** over minutes is the fingerprint; an attacker has no reason to walk the ladder upward. GNSS SV count/C/N₀ recovers in step |
| **B-04** | GM software upgrade / ISSU | Firmware push, patch window | Same as B-03 but may be hitless (no clockClass change) or a single short Announce gap; version field in inventory changes | Firmware-implant / supply-chain attack | Correlate with change management; verify image hash; compare against the *other* GMs upgraded in the same window |
| **B-05** | GM priority reconfiguration | `priority2` / `localPriority` retune during network redesign | Announce attribute change with **no** clockClass change; deterministic BMCA reselect across the whole domain at once | Priority-manipulation attack (attacker advertising priority2=0) | A legitimate change moves priority **in one direction, on a known node, once**. Attack signature is an *unknown* clockIdentity with implausibly good attributes (priority2=0 **and** clockClass 6 **and** clockAccuracy 0x20) appearing on a port that should never carry a GM |
| **B-06** | New GM added to domain | Site build, capacity expansion, redundancy uplift | New clockIdentity appears; possibly an Announce burst during commissioning; BMCA may reselect | Rogue GM insertion | Port-level expectation: does the ingress port have a *planned* GM behind it? Is the new node in the inventory/IPAM? Does SyncE ESMC QL on the same port change consistently? |
| **B-07** | GM removed / decommissioned | Site retirement, PRTC lifecycle | Clean Announce stop; BMCA falls to next-best; GM count decreases | GM DoS / link cut | Decommission ticket; the node is removed from *all* planes, not just S-plane |
| **B-08** | Dual-GM BMCA oscillation ("ping-pong") | Two GMs with **identical** clockClass/accuracy/variance/priority2 and a tie broken by a marginal path; or asymmetric announce reachability | Repeated GM identity flips at minute/second cadence; port states churn; RU `ptp-state` toggles | Intermittent rogue-master attack, or on/off Announce spoofing | Flip is **between two known GMs only** and alternates deterministically; clockIdentity tie-break (lowest identity wins) explains it. Fix is localPriority, not incident response. O-RAN WG4 troubleshooting calls this "S-1 Grandmaster flapping" |
| **B-09** | GM clockIdentity change after card/chassis swap | RMA, MAC address change | A "new" GM appears at the *same* topological position with the *same* attributes; old identity never returns | Rogue GM impersonating the real one | Physical/topological continuity: same port, same VLAN, same SyncE QL, same GNSS antenna telemetry. Asset-management correlation on serial number |
| **B-10** | GM GNSS loss → BMCA reselect to alternate GM | See §3; GM degrades below alternate | clockClass 6→7 on GM-A, downstream switches to GM-B; **path delay changes** because the new GM is a different number of hops away | Time-shift / spoofing attack causing a step | The phase step magnitude equals the known cTE difference between the two GM paths; it is **reproducible** and matches the engineering record. Attack steps are arbitrary |
| **B-11** | Announce burst / storm at commissioning | Many O-RUs/BCs powering up together after a site power restoration | Announce and Sync message rates spike above nominal (8/s, 16/s); CPU/CoPP drops on aggregation switches | Announce-flood DoS | Burst is **correlated with a power-restore event** across many nodes simultaneously and decays monotonically; source MACs are all inventoried O-RUs, not one unknown source |

## 2. Boundary clock / topology events

| ID | Scenario | Cause | Observable change | Mistakable for | Distinguisher |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B-12** | T-BC insertion into path | New aggregation hop, network growth | Hop count / `stepsRemoved` increments; cTE budget consumes another ~±X ns per G.8273.2 class; meanPathDelay changes discretely | MITM / interception device inserted in path | `stepsRemoved` change is **expected and planned**; the new BC answers management, has an inventory record, and its clockIdentity appears in Announce as a legitimate BC not a GM. An MITM typically does *not* increment stepsRemoved (it wants to be invisible) |
| **B-13** | T-BC removal / bypass | Decommission, path simplification | `stepsRemoved` decrements; meanPathDelay steps; cTE improves | — (rarely mistaken) | Planned change; improvement direction |
| **B-14** | T-BC replacement (RMA) | Hardware failure | New clockIdentity at same position; **new cTE offset** (each device has its own constant time error); brief holdover on downstream | MITM swap / hardware implant | Serial-number/asset correlation; the cTE shift is within the G.8273.2 class limit for that device type |
| **B-15** | T-BC reboot / line-card reload | Upgrade, crash, OIR | Downstream sees Announce gap → clockClass 135 (BC holdover) → 165 if holdover fails → recovery; port state churn | Targeted DoS against a BC | Whole-device symptom: all ports down together, management plane also reboots, syslog shows reload reason |
| **B-16** | Port flap / link down-up | SFP marginal, fibre cleaning, patch work, LOS | PTP port state SLAVE→LISTENING→UNCALIBRATED→SLAVE; delay_req/resp gap; ESMC also drops | Link-layer DoS, cable cut attack | Correlated L1 counters (LOS, CRC/FCS errors, optical Rx power drop) — an attack at PTP layer leaves L1 clean. Check DOM/optical power telemetry |
| **B-17** | Link protection switching (L2/L3) | LFA/FRR, TI-LFA, spanning-tree, ERPS | **Path delay steps** as the route changes; asymmetry changes; PDV profile changes. G.8261 models this as **TC-17 routing change** | Delay-attack / asymmetric-delay injection | The new meanPathDelay matches a **known alternate path** in the topology DB; IGP/LSP telemetry shows the reroute at the same timestamp; the step is bidirectional-consistent |
| **B-18** | IP/MPLS re-routing (G.8275.2 / PTS) | Metric change, link failure, TE re-optimisation | Same as B-17 but worse — PTS has no on-path support so full path PDV changes | Delay attack | Cisco explicitly warns G.8275.2 PTPoIP "can run into issues with rerouting, asymmetric routing, ECMP, bundles" — this is a *known* benign class ([Cisco best practices](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/td/docs/iosxr/ncs560/timing-and-sync/24xx/b-network-sync-24xx-ncs560/timing-design-best-practices.pdf)) |
| **B-19** | Optical / DWDM protection switch (OTN, ROADM, 1+1) | Fibre cut on working path, optical restoration | **Large, abrupt cTE step.** Coherent-optics FIFO re-lock gives random cTE **±20 to 130 ns per interface**; OTN transponder deep buffers give **±20 to 1000 ns per restart** ([Baldry, WSTS 2022](https://wsts.atis.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Jon-Baldry.A-Deep-Dive-Investigation-into-the-Challenges-of-Sync-Over-DWDM-Networks.pdf)) | Time-shift attack (sub-µs offset injection) | This is the single most common benign cause of a "clean" sub-µs step. Correlate with optical-layer alarms (LOS, protection-switch event on the OLS/ROADM). The step is **persistent and static** after the switch, not drifting |
| **B-20** | ECMP / LAG member rehash | Hash-bucket change on link add/remove, or flow-label change | Sync and Delay_Req take **different physical members** with different lengths → sudden asymmetry; PDV pattern changes | Asymmetric delay attack | Cisco and linuxptp both flag LAG/ECMP as unsupported/problematic for PTP; linuxptp has no sync-over-LAG support at all ([WSTS 2022, Reddy](https://wsts.atis.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/06-Ramana-Reddy.Challenges-with-LinuxPTP.pdf)). Check LAG member state changes at the same timestamp |
| **B-21** | BC holdover entry/exit | Upstream loss then recovery | clockClass 135 → (140/150/160) → 165; on recovery a **phase realignment** slew or step | Spoof-then-restore attack | Holdover exit is rate-limited by the servo; observable as a bounded slew, not a step, on compliant devices. **Known bug exception:** Cisco CSCvv87440 (below) makes a T-BC advertise clockClass 6 *immediately* on recovery without phase alignment |

## 3. Reference loss — GNSS, holdover, clockClass transitions

| ID | Scenario | Cause | Observable change | Mistakable for | Distinguisher |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B-22** | GNSS obstruction — foliage, new building, crane, snow/ice on radome | Site environment change | Gradual SV-count decline, C/N₀ drop, PDOP rise, then loss of fix → clockClass 6→7 | GNSS jamming | **Spectral/AGC evidence:** jamming raises receiver AGC and noise floor across the band; obstruction lowers C/N₀ *per-satellite* with elevation dependence and shows a repeating ~sidereal-day pattern. [Furuno failure factors](https://www.furuno.com/en/gnss/column/gnss-failure-factors) |
| **B-23** | GNSS antenna / feeder fault | Water ingress, connector corrosion, lightning surge, LNA DC feed open/short, rodent damage | Antenna-current alarm (open/short), abrupt total loss of all SVs, C/N₀ → 0 | GNSS jamming or antenna-cut attack | Receiver reports **antenna open/short** explicitly ([ViaLite antenna loading alarm states](https://www.vialite.com/resources/guides/gps-gnss-antenna-loading-alarm-states/), [Meinberg "Antenna Faulty"](https://kb.meinbergglobal.com/kb/antenna_faulty)). Jamming leaves antenna DC healthy with elevated AGC |
| **B-24** | GNSS multipath / urban canyon | Reflective surfaces, poor antenna siting, low sky view | Elevated C/N₀ variance, pseudorange residual spikes, timing jitter of tens of ns, intermittent fix | Spoofing (which also perturbs pseudoranges) | Multipath is **elevation/azimuth-correlated and diurnally repeating**; spoofing shows uniform, implausibly high C/N₀ across SVs and a coherent position/time shift. [Urban-canyon timing study](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1331955/FULLTEXT01.pdf) |
| **B-25** | Ionospheric scintillation / solar storm | Geomagnetic storm (e.g. May 2024 Gannon event) | Simultaneous C/N₀ fades and cycle slips at **many geographically dispersed sites**; position error excursions; some receivers lose lock | Wide-area GNSS jamming/spoofing campaign | **Geographic scale + space-weather correlation.** The May 2024 storm produced GPS position errors up to ~10 m (33 ft) across the US ([AGU/JGR](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025JA033839), [GPS World](https://www.gpsworld.com/how-the-strong-solar-storm-could-impact-gnss/)). Cross-check NOAA SWPC Kp index |
| **B-26** | Unintentional RF interference | Nearby transmitter harmonics, faulty amplifier, illegal repeater, a passing vehicle with a personal privacy device (PPD) | AGC rise, C/N₀ drop across all SVs, loss of fix — **indistinguishable from jamming at the receiver** | GNSS jamming attack | Duration and mobility: PPD/vehicle interference is transient (seconds–minutes) and recurs at traffic-correlated times; a fixed faulty emitter is continuous and direction-findable. Requires spectrum monitoring to resolve. [Septentrio](https://www.septentrio.com/en/learn-more/advanced-positioning-technology/gps-gnss-interference) |
| **B-27** | GNSS receiver self-survey restart / position-hold exit | Receiver reset, firmware update, antenna relocation, configuration change | Receiver leaves position-hold, timing accuracy degrades during survey-in (minutes–hours), then returns; brief clockClass excursion | Spoofing that drags position | Receiver mode telemetry explicitly shows "survey-in"; the surveyed position converges back to the **same** coordinates. Spoofing moves position **away** and keeps it there |
| **B-28** | GNSS space/control-segment anomaly | Bad ephemeris, bad UTC parameters, SV decommissioning bug | Time offset step of µs-scale affecting **a fraction of receivers** depending on which SV they took UTC params from | Coordinated spoofing | **The January 2016 case study:** 13 h 45 min, −13 µs, 15 of 30 SVs bad, 88.8 % of 80 NIST-monitored clocks affected ([NIST paper](https://tf.nist.gov/general/pdf/2886.pdf)). Signature: error is *identical* across affected sites globally, and correlates with SV selection, not geography |
| **B-29** | Holdover entry | Any of B-22…B-28 | clockClass 6→7; `timeTraceable` stays TRUE while in-spec; frequencyTraceable may flip; time error begins a **slow deterministic ramp** set by oscillator ageing/temperature | Slow time-shift attack | Holdover ramp is **monotonic, smooth and matches the oscillator model** (e.g. Rb ~ ns/day, OCXO ~ µs/day). A slow-drift attack need not obey the oscillator physics. Cross-check against a second, independent clock |
| **B-30** | Holdover exit / re-acquisition | GNSS returns | Phase realignment — either a bounded slew or (on non-compliant devices) a step; clockClass 52→7→6 or 248→6 | Spoof-then-restore, or attack cessation | Coincides exactly with GNSS fix restoration in receiver telemetry; the correction magnitude equals the accumulated holdover error |
| **B-31** | clockClass walk 6→7→52 (and 135→140/150/160→165 on BCs) | The canonical degradation ladder | The single most alarm-generating benign sequence in real networks | Attacker downgrading quality to force reselection | Direction and pacing. A benign walk is **downward in steps with realistic dwell times** matching the device's holdover spec, and the GM's *own* GNSS telemetry corroborates it. An attacker-forged degradation typically appears with **no corroborating GNSS/oscillator telemetry on the claimed source** |
| **B-32** | PRTC/GM oscillator holdover expiry | Extended GNSS outage beyond holdover budget | clockClass 52/165; alarms escalate; radios eventually enter their own holdover and then drop | GNSS denial attack | Purely a duration question — the *cause* is whatever caused B-22…B-28 |

## 4. Network conditions

| ID | Scenario | Cause | Observable change | Mistakable for | Distinguisher |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B-33** | Sustained congestion | Traffic growth, TM event, failed capacity plan | PDV floor and spread rise; slave wander increases; G.8261 **TC-12/TC-13/TC-14** patterns | Delay attack, timing DoS | PDV rises **with correlated interface utilisation counters** and affects *all* flows on the link, not just PTP. Check queue-depth/drop telemetry |
| **B-34** | Microbursts | Sub-second incast, TCP synchronisation, bursty U-plane | Millisecond-scale delay spikes invisible to 1-minute average counters; occasional Sync outliers | Short-duration delay injection | Requires sub-second telemetry to even see. [Arista microburst paper](https://www.arista.com/assets/data/pdf/TechBulletins/AristaMicrobursts.pdf). Bursts track application traffic patterns (business hours, batch jobs) |
| **B-35** | PTP QoS starvation / policing / CoPP drops | PTP sharing queues with U-plane, or a policer on event messages, or control-plane policing on the switch CPU | Sync/Delay_Resp loss, dTE ramps **under load only**; CoPP drop counters increment | Selective-message DoS | O-RAN WG4 troubleshooting names this "S-3 PTP QoS starvation". Verify: does dTE correlate with link utilisation? Is the drop counter a **policer**, not an unexplained gap? |
| **B-36** | Packet loss of Sync / Follow_Up / Delay_Resp | Marginal optics, CRC errors, buffer overflow | Servo starved of samples → wander; `announce_timeout` / `sync_timeout` | Selective message-dropping attack | L1 error counters (FCS, symbol errors), optical Rx power, and the fact that **non-PTP traffic also loses packets** at a proportional rate |
| **B-37** | Packet reordering | ECMP with per-packet spray, multi-lane fabrics | Sync/Follow_Up arrive out of order; sequenceId gaps that later fill; servo rejects samples | Replay attack (which also produces out-of-order sequenceIds) | Reordering keeps **each sequenceId exactly once** with correct originTimestamps and small displacement. Replay shows **duplicate sequenceIds** and/or stale originTimestamps far in the past |
| **B-38** | Packet duplication | Misconfigured mirror/SPAN, PRP/HSR, L2 loop, IGMP snooping flooding | Duplicate PTP frames with identical sequenceId and identical content | **Replay attack** — this is the classic false positive | Duplicates are **bit-identical including correctionField and arrive within microseconds**; a replay arrives with a delay and (if crafted) a modified correctionField. Also check for an L2 loop / duplicate multicast delivery path |
| **B-39** | MTU / fragmentation issues | Jumbo-frame mismatch, tunnel overhead (G.8275.2 over IP) | PTP over IP fragments or is dropped; intermittent total loss of one message type | Selective DoS | Path-MTU telemetry; fixed by MTU change; affects only the IP-encapsulated profile |
| **B-40** | Asymmetric routing | Forward and return paths differ (policy routing, hot-potato, hybrid access) | **Constant offset error = half the path-delay difference** ([Renesas](https://www.renesas.com/en/document/whp/limiting-ieee-1588-slave-clock-wander-caused-packet-delay-variation), [Meinberg](https://kb.meinbergglobal.com/kb/time_sync/time_synchronization_errors_caused_by_network_asymmetries)) | Delay-asymmetry attack | Static and reproducible; matches the measured forward/return path difference from traceroute/OAM. An attack usually varies or is unexplained by topology |
| **B-41** | Fibre length asymmetry | Unidirectional fibre pairs, repair splices of different lengths, patch-cord swaps, different drum ends | **1 m of fibre asymmetry ⇒ ~5 ns extra latency ⇒ ~2.5 ns cTE** ([Baldry, WSTS 2022](https://wsts.atis.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Jon-Baldry.A-Deep-Dive-Investigation-into-the-Challenges-of-Sync-Over-DWDM-Networks.pdf)) | Small persistent time-shift | Appears at a **maintenance event** (splice, patch, re-route) and is thereafter perfectly static. OTDR the two directions |
| **B-42** | Dispersion-compensation (DCF) asymmetry | Non-coherent DWDM with variable-length DCF spools | Variable cTE per span, direction-dependent | Time-shift | Known systemic property of the optical design; fixed by FBG modules. Static once installed |
| **B-43** | Coherent optics / OTN FIFO re-lock | Interface restart, transponder reset, optical protection event | Random cTE **±20–130 ns** (coherent) or **±20–1000 ns** (OTN muxponder) per restart | Sub-µs time-shift attack | Correlates exactly with an optical interface restart event; magnitude within the documented device range; **new value is stable until the next restart** |
| **B-44** | BiDi / CWDM single-fibre wavelength asymmetry + temperature | Two different wavelengths (e.g. 1310/1490, 1270/1330) propagate at different group velocities; chromatic dispersion is temperature-dependent | Slow diurnal cTE cycle. Measured: **220.4 ps offset change over 60 °C on 20 km BiDi vs 17.0 ps on DWDM** ([arXiv 2505.02420](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.02420)) | Slow drift attack | **Diurnal/seasonal periodicity** correlated with ambient temperature telemetry. Attack has no reason to be sinusoidal on a 24 h period |
| **B-45** | MACsec / link encryption on a timing link | Security hardening applied to a PTP-bearing interface | Timestamping accuracy degrades; Cisco states MACsec **is not supported** with SyncE or PTP on the same interface | Timing manipulation | Config audit — this is a self-inflicted, documented incompatibility ([Cisco best practices](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/td/docs/iosxr/ncs560/timing-and-sync/24xx/b-network-sync-24xx-ncs560/timing-design-best-practices.pdf)) |
| **B-46** | Multicast / VLAN / IGMP misconfiguration | Wrong PTP VLAN, IGMP snooping pruning the 01-1B-19-00-00-00 group, non-PTP-aware switch in path | Announce/Sync not received on some ports; asymmetric visibility of the GM across the domain; a BC may elect itself master | Rogue master / Announce suppression attack | Config diff; the failure is **port-scoped and matches the VLAN/IGMP topology exactly**. Cisco Nexus/Catalyst PTP troubleshooting guides list this as the leading cause of "port in MASTER when it should be SLAVE" |

## 5. Physical / environmental

| ID | Scenario | Cause | Observable change | Mistakable for | Distinguisher |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B-47** | Oscillator ageing | Quartz ageing (typ. ppb/day, decaying with a log-law), Rb drift | Very slow monotonic frequency offset growth; holdover performance degrades year on year | Long-horizon drift attack | Timescale of months–years; consistent with the device's published ageing curve; affects the device even when perfectly locked (visible only in holdover) |
| **B-48** | Temperature transient | HVAC/fan failure, cabinet door opened, direct solar load, season change | Frequency-vs-temperature converted into **frequency-vs-time ramp**. Quartz slope rises from ~10 ppb/°C (85–95 °C) to ~**3000 ppb/°C at 125 °C**; MEMS <2 ppb/°C to 105 °C ([SiTime AN10063](https://www.sitime.com/support/resource-library/application-notes/an10063-tcxo-and-ocxo-stability-degradation)) | Frequency-manipulation attack | Correlate with the device's own temperature sensor and environmental alarms. The ramp **reverses when temperature recovers** |
| **B-49** | Power cycle / oscillator retrace | Site power loss, generator transfer, breaker trip | On restart the OCXO does not return to its exact pre-shutdown frequency (retrace); warm-up excursion for minutes–hours; clockClass 248 during warm-up | DoS + spoof-on-restore | Power-alarm correlation across the whole site; multiple co-located devices show the same warm-up profile |
| **B-50** | Vibration / microphonics | Fans, HVAC compressors, nearby traffic/rail, wind on towers, construction | Elevated short-term phase noise and jitter; g-sensitivity converts vibration into frequency modulation ([Wenzel](https://wenzel.com/library/time-frequency-articles/vibration-induced-phase-noise/), [TI SNAA296](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/snaa296/snaa296.pdf)) | Jitter-injection attack | Frequency-domain signature matches a mechanical line (fan rotation rate, compressor cycle); correlates with site machinery duty cycle |
| **B-51** | Power-supply brownout / PoE budget exceeded | Marginal PSU, battery plant on discharge | Oscillator supply pulling → frequency excursion; possibly a partial reboot | Physical attack | Power telemetry; simultaneous across co-powered equipment |
| **B-52** | Humidity / condensation in enclosures | Seasonal, failed seal | Slow degradation, intermittent GNSS or optics faults | — | Site environment telemetry |

## 6. Frequency layer — SyncE / EEC / ESMC

| ID | Scenario | Cause | Observable change | Mistakable for | Distinguisher |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| **B-57** | Timing loop | Misconfigured references creating a closed frequency path | Frequency wanders off with no external reference; QL propagation reveals the loop | — | Classic misconfiguration; ESMC QL-DNU on the return path is the standard prevention |

## 10. Cross-cutting discriminators — what actually separates benign from attack

These are the recurring rules, distilled from the tables above. They are the practical basis for a look-alike classifier.

| # | Discriminator | Why it works | Benign side | Attack side |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D-1** | **Corroboration across independent layers** | An attacker usually manipulates one plane | GNSS loss shows in SV count, C/N₀, AGC, antenna current **and** clockClass **and** the oscillator's holdover ramp | Forged Announce degradation with no matching physical-layer evidence at the claimed source |
| **D-2** | **Physical-layer correlation** | L1 counters are hard to forge remotely | Port flap has LOS/FCS/optical-power evidence | PTP-layer DoS leaves L1 pristine |
| **D-3** | **Direction and pacing of quality changes** | Recovery is physically constrained | clockClass walks 248→52→7→6 upward over minutes as GNSS reacquires | Sudden jump to the most attractive attribute set |
| **D-4** | **Topological plausibility of the source** | Ports have expected roles | A new GM appears on a port that is *planned* to carry a GM | Announce arriving on an O-RU-facing port, or from an uninventoried clockIdentity |
| **D-5** | **Magnitude matched to a known mechanism** | Benign steps have engineering explanations | ±20–130 ns after a coherent-optics restart; ±20–1000 ns after an OTN restart; <1000 ns/<15 s SyncE transient; 2.5 ns per metre of fibre asymmetry; 1 s for a leap second; 37 s for a timescale flag error; 86400 s for the leap-year bug | Arbitrary offsets with no matching mechanism |
| **D-6** | **Periodicity** | Physics is periodic; attackers are not | Diurnal (temperature/BiDi dispersion), sidereal (~23 h 56 m, satellite geometry/multipath), traffic-diurnal (congestion PDV) | Aperiodic or event-triggered |
| **D-7** | **Geographic and cohort scope** | Shared causes have shared footprints | Space weather hits many dispersed sites at once; a power restore hits one cohort; a GNSS constellation anomaly hits by SV-selection not geography | Attacks concentrate on reachable network segments |
| **D-8** | **Change-management correlation** | Most "attacks" are tickets | Maintenance window, firmware batch, config diff, optical protection event, LAG member change, IGP reroute | No corresponding change record |
| **D-9** | **Duplicate vs replay** | The classic B-38 look-alike | Duplicate frames are bit-identical (including correctionField), arrive µs apart, and are explained by a mirror/loop/PRP | Replay shows stale originTimestamps, delayed arrival, and no L2 duplication path |
| **D-10** | **Reordering vs replay** | The classic B-37 look-alike | Each sequenceId appears exactly once, small displacement | Duplicate sequenceIds, or sequence regression |
| **D-11** | **Settlement blanking** | Servo convergence is not an anomaly | Suppress/discount detection for a bounded window (practitioner guidance: ≤5 min) after any legitimate BMCA/reference change | Anomaly that persists past settlement, or grows |
| **D-12** | **Holdover physics check** | Oscillators drift predictably | Holdover ramp matches the device's oscillator class and temperature history | Drift that does not obey the oscillator model |
| **D-13** | **Known-defect suppression list** | Some alarms are just bugs | Maintain a per-platform/per-release defect list (X-01…X-15) and downgrade matching signatures | Signature not on the list |
| **D-14** | **Static vs dynamic offset** | Asymmetry is constant, attacks usually are not | Fibre/DWDM/BiDi asymmetry produces a **step to a new constant value** | Time-shift attacks typically ramp, oscillate, or step repeatedly |

## 11. Known detection gaps (honest limitations, relevant to the project's negative results)

| Gap | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **GNSS receiver status is attacker-controllable** — already recorded in the project's own findings | D-1 corroboration weakens if the receiver's own telemetry is the corroborating source. Independent evidence (AGC/spectrum, second constellation, Galileo OSNMA) is required |
| **ESMC / SyncE QL is unauthenticated** | B-54 benign QL changes and forged QL changes are observationally identical without topological corroboration |
| **A "healthy-looking" spoof is indistinguishable by attribute inspection alone** | Matches the project's 0 % result on strict unseen GNSS spoof |
| **linuxptp does not reflect true device state in clockClass (X-06)** | Any detector keying on clockClass in a linuxptp-based O-DU inherits this blind spot in both directions |
| **G.8275.2 / PTS deployments legitimately behave like a delay attack** | Reroutes, ECMP and asymmetric routing are *expected*. Thresholds tuned on G.8275.1 FTS will not transfer |
| **Measurement-artefact false positives (X-13)** | Window length, filter and peak-vs-RMS choices change the verdict without any change in the network |

### Summary for the caller

- **58 benign scenarios** catalogued (B-01…B-70 with gaps where IDs are grouped), across 8 families, each with cause, observable signal, attack look-alike, and discriminator — exceeding the 40+ target.
- **15 documented vendor/implementation defects** (X-01…X-15) that are specifically known false-alarm generators, including three Cisco bug IDs with exact symptoms, and six linuxptp limitations from the WSTS 2022 Radisys presentation that directly affect any linuxptp-based O-DU testbed.
- **12 real-world incidents** (R-01…R-12) with dates, root causes and impacts. The Telstra July 2026 outage is the single most relevant: a **benign maintenance action produced a 20-year time error that downstream systems interpreted as a security anomaly** — the exact failure mode this study is about, in reverse.
- **14 cross-cutting discriminators** (D-1…D-14) distilled into a usable rule set, plus **6 honest detection gaps** that align with the project's recorded negative results (attacker-controlled GNSS status, unauthenticated ESMC, healthy-looking spoof at 0 %).
- Key quantitative anchors for threshold design: fibre asymmetry 2.5 ns cTE per metre; coherent-optics restart ±20–130 ns; OTN restart ±20–1000 ns; G.8273.2 SyncE transient <1000 ns / <15 s; transport budget ~1000 ns within an end-to-end ±1.5 µs; leap second 1 s; timescale-flag error 37 s; the 2024 Cisco leap-year bug 86400 s.
- Two notes for the project's own work: (1) the **settlement-blanking rule (D-11)** and **known-defect suppression (D-13)** are the two highest-yield false-positive reductions available and neither requires retuning a boundary on S15; (2) **G.8275.2/PTS deployments legitimately produce delay-attack-like signatures** on every reroute, so any threshold derived under G.8275.1 FTS assumptions will not transfer.
