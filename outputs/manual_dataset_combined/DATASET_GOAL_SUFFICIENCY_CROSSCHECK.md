## Corrections and tested conclusions

1. **Correction to a blanket insufficiency claim:** existing data are sufficient for bounded packet-structure/annotation description, software simulator evaluation, and some controlled software-emulation observations. User-created Netem/`ptp4l` artifacts are classified as software-collected experiments, not silently as simulation.
2. **PCAP correction:** full Announce fields were locally present but omitted from the seven-column CSV. They support an observed *advertised grandmaster-identity transition* on the c4 sender; they do not by themselves authenticate topology, prove c4's internal selection state, establish unauthorised takeover, or measure physical clock/GNSS health.
3. **No correction to the physical-evidence boundary:** the scoped artifacts do not establish a capture-specific malicious launch, physical receiver clock health, authenticated receiver source takeover, GNSS/SyncE/oscillator health, or executed physical recovery effectiveness.
4. **Why this is not merely filename-based:** `results/tier2/netem_rerun_all_ok_90.log` records actual `tc netem`, `tcpdump`, and `ptp4l` output; `docs/ENV_CAPABILITY_AUDIT.md` records software-only timestamps/no GNSS; `healing/loop.py` selects candidates while `twin/model.py` only forecasts effects. These directly set the narrower software-emulation scope.
5. **Conflict remains decisive:** aligned Announce session 1/2 non-label rows are identical but their labels conflict; neither can add independent attack ground truth or an independent train/test split.

### The pasted tables and five stated consequences, reconciled

- **Rogue-source advertisement:** supported only as b8 self-advertisement on wire; “rogue” needs authorization/topology evidence.
- **Successful takeover:** supported as an advertised-GM transition; conditional as actual parent selection; unsupported as unauthorized takeover.
- **Announce injection/identity spoofing:** the observed b8 identity differs from c4; no identity impersonation is observed. Injection and authorization remain unverified without launch record.
- **Flooding:** unsupported; matching `logAnnounceInterval=-3` does not show abnormal rate by itself.
- **Timing disruption:** advertised class/time-source changes are observed; magnitude and physical timing disruption are unmeasured.
- **Benign fault:** neither confirmed nor excluded; the advertised predecessor is not a GNSS measurement.
- **Recovery:** return of c4’s advertised GM identity is observed; no recovery action or successful outcome is recorded.
- **Needed-evidence table:** full PCAP is found locally; launch, receiver config/log, GNSS record, and action/outcome remain missing in the targeted local scope. The upstream repository/history and paper are candidates, not evidence until capture-specific material is inspected.

## Re-export assessment

Full-field export is technically valuable for preserving raw Announce fields, sourcePortIdentity, grandmasterIdentity, priorities, class, accuracy, variance, steps, timeSource, domain, and flags as a new derivative representation with PCAP hash/decoder version/packet index. It should not overwrite seven-column raw CSVs or labels. It cannot determine author intent, make zero-labelled b8 Sync/Follow_Up malicious, prove receiver health, or arbitrate labels on identical captures. A future re-export needs explicit authorization, immutable source hash binding, and a separate disposition for each derivative.

## Best defensible current project scope

The project can demonstrate a provenance-aware digital-twin/self-healing **software research workflow**: packet/PCAP parsing, derived feature construction, controlled software-network emulation, simulated H0/H1 experiments, evidence-gated use of external packet annotations, and modeled recovery-candidate ranking. It can teach why a model candidate or supplied packet label is not a measured receiver outcome.

It cannot currently claim a validated physical self-healing O-RAN deployment, a real attack detector with validated clock-health labels, a proven BMCA/source-takeover detector, GNSS/SyncE/oscillator diagnostics, or measured physical recovery success.
